## The Web Used to Be Slower

There was a time when you'd stumble across a personal website—hand-crafted HTML, a guestbook, a blogroll of friends. It was imperfect, human, and worth your time.

Today's web is optimized for engagement, scroll velocity, and ad impressions. Everything is faster, but many things are shallower.

## Long-Form Content is a Gift

Reading something that took hours to write—something with a real argument, real stakes, real vulnerability—is increasingly rare. That rarity makes it more valuable.

Letter Club is our small protest: a commitment to writing things worth reading, and reading things worth sharing.

## Meaningful Connections

When you slow down to read something carefully, you often find yourself wanting to respond. That's the web at its best—not broadcasting to an audience, but writing for a person.

## What You Can Do

- Start a blog. It doesn't need to be popular.
- Write emails instead of tweets.
- Subscribe to newsletters from writers you love.
- Reply to things. People notice.

The slow web isn't about nostalgia. It's about intention.
