Good design is invisible. When a user interface works well, people don't notice it — they simply accomplish their goals. This is the paradox at the heart of modern web design: the best work goes unnoticed.

## Clarity Over Cleverness

The temptation to be clever is real. A surprising interaction, an unexpected animation, a clever metaphor. But clarity should always win. Users don't want to think about your interface — they want to use it.

A clear interface has:

- **Obvious affordances** — buttons look like buttons, links look like links.
- **Predictable behavior** — actions have consistent, expected outcomes.
- **Minimal cognitive load** — less to remember, fewer decisions to make.

## Whitespace Is Not Empty Space

Designers who fear whitespace fill every pixel. But whitespace is an active design element. It creates breathing room, establishes hierarchy, and guides the eye. Cramped interfaces feel anxious; spacious interfaces feel calm.

## Typography as the Foundation

Before you think about color, before you design a single component, think about typography. A well-chosen typeface with a well-considered scale can carry an entire design. Everything else becomes decoration.

> "Typography is the craft of endowing human language with a durable visual form." — Robert Bringhurst

## Consistency Compounds

Every inconsistency in an interface is a small tax on the user's attention. Over hundreds of interactions, these taxes compound. Consistent spacing, consistent naming, consistent behavior — they signal care and build trust.

## Conclusion

Modern web design isn't about trends. It's about reducing friction, building trust, and respecting the user's time and attention. Start with clarity, add space, get the type right, and stay consistent. The rest will follow.
